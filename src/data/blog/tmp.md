Storms, particularly hurricanes, typhoons, cyclones, and severe thunderstorms, are becoming more intense due to a fundamental change in the Earth's climate.

Climate change is not linked to the increment in the number of storms worldwide. However, warmer oceans and atmosphere make this extreme weather condition more intense.

## Table of Contents

## Warmer Oceans

In recent years, average sea temperature have hit high records. For storms to form, moist air rises from the ocean surface, winds begin to spin, which generally needs the sea surface to be at least 27°C.

Ocean warming now extends hundreds of feet deep. Usually, a storm's churning brings up cold water from below, which acts as a "brake" on its strength. Now, storms are pulling up more warm water, allowing them to maintain peak intensity for longer period.

## Wetter Atmosphere

For every 1°C rise in temperature, the atmosphere can hold around 7% more water vapor. This allows storms to move more water from the ocean to the land, which, in recent years, causes tropical storms to have record-breaking in rainfall.
